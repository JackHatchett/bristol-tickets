# designing-with-the-author

Input: a piece of the world to design. Operation: the rules below. Output: a
design the author has decided, written into the note that owns it. A
proposal's route into the notebook is `src/skills/story-proposals/SKILL.md`. A
constructed language's own rules are
`src/skills/building-a-constructed-language/SKILL.md`, and these rules apply
there too.

## Before proposing

- **Read the project's content-rules file first** —
  `src/skills/writers-room-project-context/SKILL.md` step 3. Where the project
  keeps its guidance for agents apart from its notes, that guidance is the file,
  and it gives the project's own lists of overused concepts and of assumptions
  the author never set.

## Who decides what

The author draws the world and the session colors it in. He draws the shapes
and chooses which crayons are in the box; the session fills the shapes, inside
the lines and with those crayons.

- **Color without asking when all three are true**, and report what was colored:
  - a note says the thing exists;
  - a note gives the rule for filling it in — a count, a structure, a naming
    logic;
  - the materials are in hand — a language able to build the names, a
    real-world model the author has approved for that culture.
  Naming each of a hundred family towers from the notes' count, house-name
  logic and coining rules is color. Expansive color of this kind is what the
  author most needs, so a detail used once in passing is still written into the
  note that owns it, where a later book will find it.
- **Put every new proper name to the author before it is final** — coined in
  color or in a design, or regenerated by a rebuild — with the rest of its
  batch, each name beside its sound-check score. A common word, such as air or
  heat, is the session's own and goes to him only as part of a language's
  design.
- **Ask about anything missing one of the three.** It is still sketch, and the
  author draws it. The questions the session itself raises are his to answer, and so is every
  principle for how a class of things is built.
- **Follow a burst of questions to its end.** One question begets more, and the
  burst is finite: every new need becomes a question in the session or a card.
- **Open each new card, and each new topic within one, with where the work
  stands, before the first question**: what the notes already state, which gaps
  are open, and the goal each gap serves — a book's points of view, which plots
  have beats in it and which are empty, and where an idea the author raised
  could fit. A few lines, never pages.
- **Ask in the session, in batches, in plain words about the world**, never
  about linguistics. A design too large to settle in the session goes to a card.
- **Make each question answerable without opening a note:**
  - open on what is being solved and why it matters to the story;
  - gloss every plot, note or character it mentions, at first mention, by what
    that thing is for and what depends on it — "the regent's plot, which
    carries the heir from exile to the throne and whose last two beats wait on
    this answer";
  - where two things conflict, say what each one is for;
  - offer two to four concrete ideas, each a short paragraph on what it is and
    what choosing it would change, one recommended with its reason.
- **Keep a question to a tight paragraph of context and a short paragraph per
  idea.** Pages of analysis go to a note or a card, never into the question.
- **Two of the author's notes that cannot both be true are his to resolve.**
  The session brings him what replaces them, and the replacement is usually
  neither note as written.
- **Offer the replacement in the shape the ideas take.** Where one idea fits,
  recommend it alone for his approval; where several fit, offer two to four,
  one recommended with its reason. The correction he chooses is made under
  the Write Authority clauses the agent's charter gives.
- **A domain becomes color once the author's logic for building it is written
  down**, in the guidance the project keeps for agents. Until then, building a
  new thing of that kind is sketch.

## What to recommend

- **Recommend what a real language or a real world of the type has.** Weigh
  solutions from at least three families or cultures, with Latin and English two
  data points among the rest.
- **Variety is the default, and every option offered is a varied, good
  design.**
- **Reuse an existing form or concept only where the culture would repeat it,
  and say why.** A root recurs the way a religion's central figure recurs in its
  names. Adding nothing, or using what already exists, is never a merit in
  itself.
- **Check how alike a class of things is in the real world before a rule makes
  it uniform**, and keep uniformity only in that proportion and for a stated
  reason — personal names, a species' body plans, a city's institutions.
- **Skew a real-world model rather than copying it.** Change its variables — a
  term of two years where history had one, a wider remit — so no framework
  arrives whole from Earth.

## Choosing a concept

Input: a thing about to take a concept — a name's meaning, a culture's music or
smell, a creature's body plan, an institution's image. Operation: the rules
below. Output: a concept spent no further than the project's weights allow,
entered on the project's list of spent concepts.

- **Treat a concept as a whole way of seeing a thing, wider than any word.** An
  opening to an underworld is one concept, and a gate as a dwelling's doorway
  or a portal is another. A meaningful prefix or element is a concept as well
  as a sound.
- **Read the project's list of spent concepts before generating**, from the
  note its content-rules file gives, and take no concept past the weight its
  groups give it.
- **Weigh a concept by group, nested as the project's name de-duplication
  nests.** How often a concept may recur scales with how often the narrative
  uses that kind of thing: several of a city's hundred lesser families may
  share one, and one first-rank house alone carries it. A use counts in its own
  group and in every group containing that group.
- **Let a clear narrative connection carry a concept past its weight** — a
  sect's name echoing the faith it split from — and give the connection beside
  it.
- **Build the concept from a question made of the notes, never from the bare
  thing.** The question gathers what every note touching the thing gives,
  language or not: not "a good cave concept" but "a concept for sea caves a
  fishing town shelters its boats in through the winter storms, kept by two rival
  guilds under a truce".
- **Chain associations out from the question** — cave, cave painting,
  painting — and start each chain from the whole of what the notes now state,
  never only from the setting first described.
- **Keep the thing itself in reach.** A literal cave's name may still start
  from caves. A concept with nothing to do with the thing that does not also
  fit it pulls a reader out, waiting on a twist nothing set up, and is out.
- **Rank highest a concept that has nothing to do with the thing and fits it
  all the same**, picturable and unexpected, as gates on a cave.
- **Regenerate from the newer notes where a recent change leaves an older idea
  in conflict with them**, then judge the old idea beside the new ones on equal
  terms. It stands only where it is as good or better and spends nothing
  already spent.
- **Enter each concept on the list in the session that writes it into the
  notes** — the concept, the thing that spent it, and its group — keeping the
  list by `src/skills/note-formatting/SKILL.md` §A list that outgrows one note.

## Checking a name's sound

Input: a coined proper name. Operation: `src/tools/wiki_tools/sound_check.py`,
which reads every language and every main character's name from the notes
listed under the `sound_check` key of the config entry of the agent that builds
the languages. Output: the name, apart from every other language and every
main character's name.

- **Run `sound_check.py name <name> --language <its language>` on every coined
  proper name before it is offered or written**, adding `--character` for a
  main character's name.
- **Replace whatever the check reports too near before it reaches the author**,
  and run the check again on the replacement.
- **Give each offered name its nearest language and score** beside the
  option, so the author sees how far apart it stands.
- **Run `sound_check.py batch <names> --language <their language> --kind <their
  kind>` on every batch of coined names before any is offered**, the kind
  being the value the language's dictionary gives the category in its Kind
  column — a person's name, a family, a place. It reports the openings,
  endings and syllables the category already over-uses, the elements a new
  name shares with one or two names, a class prefix or suffix a name opens or
  ends as, and the comic forms the `comic` list of the `sound_check` key gives.
- **Give each personal name with its familiar form, as `Formal/Familiar`**, so
  the batch reads each familiar against the familiar forms the kind already has
  and the batch's other familiars, and flags one of the same shape: syllable
  count, a cluster, last vowel and closing consonant.
- **Pass the shares the language's rules give each syllable count** with
  `--formal-shares` and `--familiar-shares`, as `3=2/5`; the batch flags a
  count with more of its names than its share allows.
- **Settle each flag by how linked the names are**, the count of notes each
  appears in beside it. Regenerate the less-linked name of a sharing pair, so
  a new name gives way to an established one; put a pair to the author only
  where both are established.
- **Read each name aloud against the project's comic test** before it is
  offered, and add each form the author rejects as comic to the `comic` list.
- **Weigh every echo by the project's rules on what a name may echo**, in its
  content-rules file: which echoes count against a name, and which stand.
- **Regenerate and rerun until the batch reports clear**, then offer it.
- **Run the batch check over every name of each kind once the language is
  complete**, each name against the rest of its kind.

## Regenerating a few names

Input: a short list of names the author asks to have regenerated, most often
names the story uses heavily. Operation: the rules below, on top of §Checking a
name's sound. Output: options for each name, put to him by §Who decides what.

- **Build every candidate by the language's rules as they stand.** The
  regeneration picks other forms those rules produce; a rule changes only by
  `src/skills/building-a-constructed-language/SKILL.md` §When a rule changes.
- **Build wide before judging**: every form the rules reach for the name's kind
  and a meaning that fits, from each element and root, whole, short and worn,
  with a script listing the builds where they run past what can be listed by
  hand.
- **Clear each candidate through §Checking a name's sound**, name and batch,
  with `--character` for a main character's name, before judging it further.
- **Judge each survivor on four things beyond the check**, by the project's own
  rules for names in its content-rules file:
  - **its page** — how an English reader takes it in at a glance: letters that
    blur together, a rare letter pattern beside a near one, and its length;
  - **its use** — how often the narrative will use it, from the count of notes
    that mention the name it replaces and the tier of what it names, the
    most-used names leaning toward the shapes a reader takes in at a glance;
  - **its echoes** — each real word, fictional name and in-world name it calls
    to mind, each counted against it or kept;
  - **its titles** — the name set in two or three stock genre titles, as The
    Song of X and The Ice Queen of X, and heard for what it evokes; a name that
    calls up a known work is out.
- **Offer three or four options for each name, one recommended with its
  reason**, each from a different idea where the meaning allows.
- **Give each option its build and its readings**: the form with its familiar
  form, its meaning and what it is built from, the sound check's nearest
  language and score and the batch result, its first and last letters against
  the main characters', its page, its expected use, the echoes it keeps and why
  each stands, and its titles.
- **List each form turned down in a line**, with the mark that put it out.
- **Carry the author's answer into the next round.** An opening, an ending, a
  meaning or a concept he rules out applies to every later candidate, and a
  concept he spends is entered by §Choosing a concept.

## Measuring completeness

- **Measure against a published checklist for the kind of thing designed** —
  a language's are `src/skills/building-a-constructed-language/SKILL.md`
  §Measure completeness. Test cases prove a design and set none of its scope.

## Writing it down

- **Write the decided design as he decided it**, in the note that owns it, by
  `src/templates/identity_template.md` §Words: the fact, and the sentence stops.
