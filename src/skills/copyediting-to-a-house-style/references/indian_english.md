# indian_english — Indian English publishing

Builds on: new_harts

Indian English book publishing has no national style manual. Its publishers
work in British (Oxford) conventions, set out in `new_harts.md`, and their
house style guides depart from them at the points below, which replace or add
to the rules of `new_harts.md` for this style. Each rule here is stated in this
file's own words and carries the ID a flag cites.

Sources: the Pratham Books Style Guide (CC BY), which prefers British and
Indian spellings, double quotation marks for speech, and lakh and crore with
Indian digit grouping; and the stylesheet of Sahitya Akademi's journal
*Indian Literature*, which sets British *-ise* spelling, double marks for
quoted matter, single marks within, and day-month-year dates.

## Replacing rules of new_harts

- **IND-P5 Quotation marks** (replaces NHR-P5). Double marks for speech and
  quoted matter, single marks for a quotation inside one; in dialogue the
  closing punctuation of the spoken sentence goes inside.
- **IND-H3 Spelling** (replaces NHR-H3). British forms. Verbs take *-ize* or
  *-ise* by the project style sheet's choice, *-ise* where the sheet sets
  none; *analyse* and *paralyse* in either.
- **IND-N1 Words or numerals** (replaces NHR-N1). Numbers below ten are spelled
  out; 10 and above take numerals, except in dialogue, where a number stays
  in words wherever that stays readable.

## Additional rules

- **IND-N7 Lakh and crore.** Indian sums of money and large counts in an Indian
  setting are given in lakh (100,000) and crore (10,000,000), with a space
  before the unit: *Rs 12 lakh*, *34 crore*. Foreign currencies take Western
  numbering.
- **IND-N8 Digit grouping.** Numerals written in lakh and crore group
  Indian-style (*1,00,000*); the project style sheet may set Western grouping
  instead, and the rule is then consistency with it.
- **IND-C4 Honorifics.** Indian honorifics and forms of address (*ji*, *sahib*,
  *Shri*, *Smt*) keep the form the project style sheet gives; *Shri* and
  *Smt* take no point.
- **IND-I4 Indian-language words.** Italic unless the Oxford dictionaries
  treat the word as naturalized (*dhoti*, *sari*, *chapati* stay roman); a
  word the text uses often is italic at each use unless the style sheet sets
  it roman.
