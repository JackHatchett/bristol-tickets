# new_harts — New Hart's Rules, with the Oxford dictionaries

UK publishing in Oxford style. Spelling follows the first form the *Oxford
Dictionary of English* gives, in Oxford spelling; everything else follows the
rules below, each stated in this file's own words and carrying the ID a flag
cites. *New Hart's Rules* itself is the authority where a case falls outside
them, and a flag on such a case says so.

## Punctuation

- **NHR-P1 Serial comma.** In a list of three or more, a comma goes before the
  conjunction: *bread, salt, and wine*.
- **NHR-P2 Spaced en dash.** A parenthetical dash is an en dash (–) with a
  space either side: *the gate – shut since spring – stood open*. A closed em
  dash or a hyphen standing in for one is a departure.
- **NHR-P3 En dash for ranges.** Closed up between numbers and dates: *pages
  12–19*, *1990–4*. A range introduced by *from* or *between* takes words.
- **NHR-P4 Ellipsis.** Three full points, spaced from the words either side,
  with no fourth point where a sentence ends.
- **NHR-P5 Quotation marks.** Single marks, with double marks for a quotation
  inside one. In dialogue the closing punctuation of the spoken sentence goes
  inside the closing mark; elsewhere a mark goes inside only when it belongs to
  the quoted matter.
- **NHR-P6 Independent clauses.** Two independent clauses joined by *and*,
  *but*, *or*, *so* or *yet* take a comma before the conjunction unless both
  are short and closely tied. Two joined by a comma alone are a comma splice;
  in dialogue and close interior voice a splice the author chose is queried
  rather than corrected.
- **NHR-P7 Possessives.** Singular nouns take *'s*, names ending in *s*
  included (*James's*); classical and biblical names ending in *s* take the
  apostrophe alone (*Socrates'*).

## Dialogue

- **NHR-D1 New speaker, new paragraph.**
- **NHR-D2 Tags.** *'Go,' she said.* — the tag begins lowercase after the
  closing mark; an action beat is its own sentence.
- **NHR-D3 Interruption and trailing off.** Speech cut off takes a closed-up
  en dash or a spaced one by the sheet's choice, used consistently; speech that
  trails away takes an ellipsis (NHR-P4).

## Numbers and dates

- **NHR-N1 Words or numerals.** Whole numbers from one to one hundred, and
  round numbers, are spelled out in running prose; other numbers take
  numerals.
- **NHR-N2 Sentence openings.** A number beginning a sentence is spelled out,
  or the sentence is recast.
- **NHR-N3 Centuries and decades.** Centuries spelled out in lowercase (*the
  twenty-first century*); decades in numerals with no apostrophe (*the 1990s*).
- **NHR-N4 Times.** Spelled out in running prose (*half past four*); with
  numerals, *a.m.* and *p.m.* lowercase with points.
- **NHR-N5 Dates.** Day, month, year with no comma: *20 November 2026*.
- **NHR-N6 Percentages.** *Per cent*, two words, in running text.

## Capitals

- **NHR-C1 Titles of people.** Capitalized before a name, lowercase alone or
  after it.
- **NHR-C2 Kinship terms.** Capitalized as a name or in address, lowercase
  with a possessive.
- **NHR-C3 Titles of works.** Capitalize the first word and every word but
  articles, conjunctions and prepositions.

## Italics

- **NHR-I1 Titles of long works.** Italic for books, films, periodicals, long
  poems and ships; roman in single quotation marks for chapters, short stories
  and songs.
- **NHR-I2 Foreign words.** Italic for a word the Oxford dictionaries do not
  treat as naturalized; roman for one they do. Words of a project's invented
  languages follow the project style sheet.
- **NHR-I3 Emphasis.** Italics, sparingly.

## Hyphenation, spelling and abbreviation

- **NHR-H1 Compound modifiers.** Hyphenated before the noun, usually open
  after it; never with an adverb in *-ly*.
- **NHR-H2 Prefixes.** Hyphenated where the prefix ends in the vowel the stem
  begins with (*re-enter*, *pre-empt*), before a capital or numeral, or where
  the closed form misreads; closed otherwise.
- **NHR-H3 Oxford spelling.** British forms (*colour*, *centre*, *grey*,
  *travelled*, *judgement*, *ageing*) with *-ize* in verbs that take it
  (*realize*, *organize*) and *-yse* in *analyse*, *paralyse*.
- **NHR-H4 Contractions and abbreviations.** A contraction ending in the
  word's last letter takes no point (*Mr*, *Mrs*, *Dr*, *St*); an abbreviation
  that cuts the word short takes one (*Prof.*, *e.g.*, *i.e.*). *US* and *UK*
  without points.

## Word choice

- **NHR-W1 *That* and *which*.** *Which* may introduce a restrictive clause;
  a restrictive *which* is not a departure.
