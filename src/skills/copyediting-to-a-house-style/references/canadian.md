# canadian — Editing Canadian English, with the Canadian Oxford Dictionary

Builds on: chicago

Canadian book publishing in the approach of *Editing Canadian English*
(Editors Canada, 3rd edition), with the *Canadian Oxford Dictionary* (2nd
edition) for spelling. Canadian trade publishing works in North American
conventions, set out in `chicago.md`, and departs from them at the points
below, which replace or add to the rules of `chicago.md` for this style. Each
rule here is stated in this file's own words and carries the ID a flag cites.
*Editing Canadian English* leaves many choices to the house; where it does,
the rule says the project style sheet decides.

Sources: Editors Canada, *Editing Canadian English*, 3rd edition, and its
national style sheet (Canadian Oxford Dictionary for spelling, *Editing
Canadian English* for style); the Translation Bureau's Writing Tips on
spelling variations and on writing the date (our-languages.canada.ca).

## Replacing rules of chicago

- **CAN-P1 Serial comma** (replaces CMS-P1). The comma before the final *and*
  or *or* in a list of three or more is a house choice. Where the project
  style sheet sets none, keep it, as Chicago does; where the sheet drops it,
  it goes in only where the list would misread without it.
- **CAN-N5 Dates** (replaces CMS-N5). Month, day, year (*November 20, 2026*),
  with a comma after the year when the sentence continues; the project style
  sheet may set day, month, year (*20 November 2026*), which takes no commas.
  A date given as month and year alone takes no comma.
- **CAN-H3 Spelling** (replaces CMS-H3). The first spelling the *Canadian
  Oxford Dictionary* gives. In practice: *-our* (*colour*, *honour*,
  *neighbour*, but *coloration*, *honorary*); *-re* (*centre*, *theatre*,
  *metre*); *-ize* and *-yze* (*realize*, *organize*, *analyze*); a doubled *l*
  before a suffix (*travelled*, *counselled*, *marvellous*); *-ce* for the
  nouns *defence*, *licence* and *practice*, with *license* and *practise* as
  verbs; *cheque*, *catalogue*, *grey*, *program*; and the American forms
  Canadians use (*curb*, *tire*).

## Additional rules

- **CAN-N7 Measurements.** Metric units carry Canadian spelling (*kilometre*,
  *litre*). Imperial units Canadians still use in speech and daily life (a
  person's height and weight, a recipe) are left as written in dialogue and
  close voice, and queried elsewhere when the project style sheet sets metric.
