# mla — MLA Handbook, 9th edition, with Merriam-Webster

Builds on: chicago

Scholarly and non-fiction prose in the style of the *MLA Handbook* (Modern
Language Association, 9th edition, 2021), with *Merriam-Webster* for
spelling. MLA prose works in the US conventions set out in `chicago.md`
(serial comma, double quotation marks, the closed em dash, headline-style
titles) and departs from them at the points below, which replace or add to
the rules of `chicago.md` for this style. Each rule here is stated in this
file's own words and carries the ID a flag cites. The Handbook itself is the
authority where a case falls outside them, and a flag on such a case says so.
Works-cited entries and in-text citations are the Handbook's documentation
system and are outside this file.

Sources: the *MLA Handbook*, 9th edition, on numbers, dates, times and
abbreviations, as its rules are set out in university writing-centre guides
adapted from it (Abilene Christian University's MLA 9 Quick Guide, Herkimer
College's MLA 9 Formatting Guidelines, Pasco-Hernando State College's
Writing Center).

## Replacing rules of chicago

- **MLA-N1 Words or numerals** (replaces CMS-N1). A number that can be written
  in one or two words is spelled out (*eight*, *thirty-six*, *fifteen hundred*,
  *three million*); any other takes numerals (*137*, *2½*, *1,275*). Numbers
  compared in one sentence take one form (*5 out of 125*). Compound numbers
  and fractions in words are hyphenated (*forty-five*, *two-fifths*). In
  prose with many numbers, the project style sheet may set numerals
  throughout, and the rule is then consistency with it.
- **MLA-N4 Times** (replaces CMS-N4). Numerals on the twelve-hour clock, *a.m.*
  and *p.m.* lowercase with periods (*2:15 p.m.*). In dialogue a time the
  author wrote in words is a voice choice and stands.
- **MLA-N5 Dates** (replaces CMS-N5). Day, month, year (*20 November 2026*), with
  no commas, or month, day, year (*November 20, 2026*) with a comma after the
  year when the sentence continues; one order throughout, by the project
  style sheet where it sets one. No ordinal on the day (*April 6*, not *April
  6th*). Month names are not abbreviated in running prose.
- **MLA-N6 Percentages and money** (replaces CMS-N6). Spelled out where the
  whole can be written in three words or fewer (*nine percent*, *thirty-five
  dollars*); numerals with the symbol otherwise (*48.5%*, *$5.35*). Words and
  a symbol are never mixed (*9%* or *nine percent*, never *nine %*).
- **MLA-H4 Abbreviations** (replaces CMS-H4). No periods or spaces in an
  abbreviation made chiefly of capitals (*US*, *PhD*, *BA*); *Mr.*, *Mrs.*,
  *Dr.* keep theirs, and lowercase abbreviations take periods (*a.m.*,
  *e.g.*).
