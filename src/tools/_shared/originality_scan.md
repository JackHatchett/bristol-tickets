# originality_scan — shared capability

Scan a document, or a set of documents, for material borrowed from an existing
work. Input: the text and whatever touchstones the work states for itself.
Output: one entry per passage at risk.

## The line: inspiration vs. infringement

- **Borrowable (mood):** a register, a rhythm, a mood taken from a stated
  inspiration touchstone. A work's own notes name its touchstones — read what
  the project actually calls that file rather than assuming a name.
- **Not borrowable (content):** a named character, a specific plot device, a
  signature line, a distinctive creature or world design, a recognisable
  structure. The thing itself, not its mood.

## The four tests

Apply each to every passage carrying a name, a character, a beat or a design.

1. **Name** — does this name belong to a known character, place, brand or work?
   Unsure counts as at risk.
2. **Device** — is this plot or puzzle device lifted from a specific known
   story, or built from the work's own established mechanics? The latter is the
   divergence: derive from its own logic rather than a genre cliché.
3. **Phrase** — is any line a recognisable quote or paraphrase? The divergence
   is the same sentence in the work's own voice.
4. **Silhouette** — does a character or creature design read as a known IP's?
   Diverge the silhouette, the palette or the defining feature.

## The report

One entry per passage that failed a test:

- **The passage**, quoted or located precisely.
- **What it resembles**, named — the work, and the element inside it.
- **A concrete divergence**, specific enough to apply without a second pass.

A near miss is reported. A false alarm costs one line to dismiss; an unreported
resemblance costs the work. What the caller does with the report is theirs.
